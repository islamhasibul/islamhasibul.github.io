Reducing Notification Fatigue using a Portable Adaptive-Baseline PM2.5 Monitor for Persistently Polluted Cities

Preprint Submitted to the 29th International Conference on Computer and Information Technology (ICCIT 2026)

A portable ESP32-C3 PM2.5 monitor that alerts only on statistically significant spikes above a sliding personal baseline instead of a fixed threshold. In a two-week field study in Dhaka, this cut alerts from dozens per day to a few, and the alerts that remained were far more meaningful.

Paper PosterPhoto
Abstract

Air pollution is a serious concern, particularly in cities like Dhaka where the air is almost never clean. Monitors with a fixed limit send too many alerts and users stop paying attention, which defeats the purpose. A portable system was built with an ESP32-C3, a PMS7003 and a BME280, soldered onto a perfboard and powered by one 18650 battery. The firmware keeps a sliding history of recent PM2.5 readings and only alerts on a statistically significant spike above the baseline. Alerts go to a smartphone over Bluetooth Low Energy. A React Native app and a Firebase backend were made to show live data and log spike events. One of the authors carried the device for two weeks in Dhaka, the first week in fixed-threshold mode and the second in spike-detection mode. The fixed-threshold mode produced dozens of alerts per day while spike-based mode only generated a few. The spike alerts were found to be considerably more meaningful, suggesting adaptive alerting can reduce alert desensitisation even in a heavily polluted city.

MIME: Multi-Strategy Imbalance Mitigation Engine for Skin Cancer Classification

Conference 7th International Conference on Trends in Computational and Cognitive Engineering (TCCE 2025), Universiti Teknikal Malaysia Melaka, 12–13 November 2025

A unified framework comparing sampling, loss re-weighting and DC-GAN augmentation for class imbalance on ISIC 2019, built on an EfficientNetV2-S backbone. Generative augmentation raised macro-F1 from 60.42% to 82.84% and squamous cell carcinoma F1 from 11.89% to 53.18%.

Paper Certificate
Abstract

Severe class imbalance is a major obstacle in automated skin lesion diagnosis, where minority classes such as squamous cell carcinoma are often underdetected. We propose MIME, a unified framework that evaluates three complementary strategies: sampling to rebalance the training pool, loss functions to adjust learning priorities, and DC-GAN augmentation to enrich minority cases. Built on an EfficientNetV2-S backbone with custom classifier heads and optional channel attention, the MIME framework provides a controlled setting to assess each method individually and in combination. Experiments on the ISIC 2019 dataset demonstrate that sampling and loss based adjustments yield moderate benefits, but generative augmentation delivers the most substantial improvements. Compared to a linear baseline, accuracy increased from 82.25% to 90.08%, macro F1 increased from 60.42% to 82.84%, and SCC F1 improved dramatically from 11.89% to 53.18%. These results highlight the importance of targeted strategies for minority class enhancement, showing that DC-GAN based augmentation can significantly boost sensitivity to aggressive but underrepresented lesions while maintaining strong performance on majority classes. Through a unified and reproducible protocol, MIME provides a practical foundation for developing transparent, more robust, and clinically reliable dermatology AI systems.

Federated Deep Learning for Alzheimer's Disease Detection: Strong Internal Performance Does Not Survive a Change of Imaging Source

Md. Hasibul Islam, et al.

In preparation Computer Vision course research; manuscript prepared for submission to a Springer Nature journal

A dual-channel ConvNeXt-Small + Swin-V2-S classifier with CBAM fusion, trained centrally and with simulated federated learning (FedAvg, FedProx, FedBN). It reaches 92.74% internal accuracy, but accuracy falls to 12–22% on 86,437 external OASIS-1-derived images. Strong same-source results did not show that the model is robust across imaging sources.

Paper
Abstract

Deep learning can stage Alzheimer's disease from magnetic resonance imaging, but performance on held-out images from the training source may not generalize across imaging collections. We developed a dual-channel classifier combining ConvNeXt-Small and Swin-V2-S representations with Convolutional Block Attention Module fusion. The executed objective used soft-target cross-entropy and focal loss; a configured supervised contrastive term was numerically inactive. A 6,400-image four-class training collection lacked subject identifiers and was split at image level. We compared centralized training with federated averaging, federated proximal optimization and a federated batch-normalization-style strategy using two simulated same-source clients. Evaluation comprised 951 internal images and 86,437 OASIS-1-derived external images. Internally, the batch-normalization-style model achieved 92.74% accuracy, 95.13% balanced accuracy, a macro-F1 score of 0.945 and a macro area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.991. Externally, accuracy fell to 12.06–22.11%, macro-F1 to 0.097–0.173 and macro area under the curve to 0.634–0.692. Raw source statistics differed substantially, with Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics of 0.967 and 0.997 and MMD² = 0.534. Temperature scaling increased validation expected calibration error from 0.348 to 0.390, and the batch-normalization-style model produced 9,711 high-confidence external errors compared with 10 internally. Strong same-source performance and simulated federated training therefore did not establish cross-source robustness. Without subject identifiers, internal performance must be interpreted as an image-level upper-bound estimate rather than evidence of subject-independent generalization.
